Ancient Civilization

The ancient builders are known only as:

The Lumenwrights

This is not necessarily their true name. It is the name players, lore fragments, and advancements use for them.

The Lumenwrights were an advanced alien civilization that did not use fire, redstone, or conventional machinery. Their technology was based on:

living light
crystal resonance
gravity manipulation
biological circuits
luminous alloys
memory stored in crystals
structures grown and built together

They did not separate nature from technology. Their cities were part architecture, part machine, part organism.

By the time the player arrives, the Lumenwrights are gone. Only ruins remain.

There are no living Lumenwright NPCs.

Core Visual Identity

A Vestige City looks like an alien city that has been dead for thousands of years.

It contains:

broken glowing brick roads
collapsed towers
cracked domes
crescent-shaped arches
floating fragments
empty transit channels
overgrown plazas
shattered crystal pylons
dead machines embedded in walls
roots growing through old civic buildings
luminous power conduits that still flicker weakly

The architecture should be obviously not human.

No square medieval houses.
No villages.
No castles.
No normal streets.

The city is built around circular plazas, radial paths, crescent arches, vertical spires, and impossible-looking platforms.

Minecraft blocks are square, but the design language should imply curves through stepped rings, diagonals, terraces, and layered geometry.

New Material: Glowbrick

Glowbrick is the signature building material of Vestige Cities.

It is a luminous alien brick made from refined Lumenwilds ore.

Crafting Glowbricks

Glowbrick is crafted from Luminite Ingots.

Recommended recipe:

L I L
I C I
L I L

Where:

I = Luminite Ingot
C = Lumen Crystal Shard
L = Glow Pollen

Output:

4 Glowbrick Blocks

This makes Glowbrick feel advanced, luminous, and native to the dimension.

Glowbrick Properties

Glowbrick is not as bright as a lantern. It is architectural light.

Recommended behavior:

Emits light level 6
Stronger than faint grass glow
Weaker than Lumen Lanterns
Mineable with pickaxe
Blast resistance higher than stone
Has a faint animated shimmer if feasible later

Cracked Glowbrick emits light level 3.

Ancient Glowbrick emits light level 1–2, as if its energy is almost dead.

This lets city ruins visibly fade from bright intact areas to dying old sections.

City Generation Philosophy

Vestige Cities should be sporadic and rare.

They are a major discovery, not common scenery.

Rarity

They should generate less often than villages and less often than ruined portals.

A player exploring normally should feel lucky to find one.

Recommended rarity:

Small ruins: uncommon but not constant
Medium cities: rare
Grand city ruins: very rare
Sunken/overgrown variants: rare biome-specific variants
Biome Placement

Vestige Cities can generate in:

Lumen Glade
Glowroot Forest
Sporefall Jungle
Glasspetal Crags
Stillbloom Basin, rarely
Undercrown Caverns as buried city fragments

They should not dominate every biome.

Terrain Adaptation

Cities conform to terrain in a broken way.

They are not perfectly flat. They should appear partially swallowed by the landscape.

Common placements:

half-buried into hills
split by ravines
pierced by Glowroot trees
flooded by Lumenwater
suspended on broken pylons
partially collapsed into caves
overrun by Sporefall Jungle

The best city ruins feel like the world reclaimed them.

City Layout

Each Vestige City is generated from modular pieces.

Main Components
1. Arrival Edge

The player usually finds the edge first.

This area has:

broken glowbrick road segments
toppled pillars
scattered debris
overgrown wall fragments
cracked light conduits
low-value loot

This tells the player they are approaching something important.

2. Outer Ring

The outer ring contains collapsed residential or civic shells.

Structures include:

broken crescent houses
hollow pod-like buildings
cracked archways
root-filled chambers
empty plinths
dead garden terraces

Most buildings here are empty or lightly looted.

3. Central Plaza

Every Vestige City has a central plaza.

The plaza contains:

circular or octagonal layout
chiseled glowbrick floor
large central monument
dead crystal fountain or dry Lumenwater basin
several radial paths
flickering light pylons

The central plaza is the visual anchor.

The player should be able to stand here and think:

This was once the heart of a city.

4. Spire District

Most cities have at least one broken spire.

Spire features:

tall narrow tower
shattered upper floors
floating debris chunks
exposed crystal core
stair fragments
dangerous vertical traversal
better loot near the top or inside the base

The spire is where advanced technology is most visible.

5. Substructure

Below the city is a buried technical layer.

This contains:

conduit tunnels
maintenance chambers
broken energy channels
sealed vault rooms
dead machine blocks
crystal memory chambers
hostile mobs

This is the dungeon-like part.

The surface ruin is exploration.
The underground ruin is risk and reward.

Advanced Technology Remnants

The city ruins must show evidence of advanced technology without turning into sci-fi computers.

The Lumenwrights’ technology looks biological, crystalline, and architectural.

1. Dead Lumen Conduits

These are thin glowing strips embedded in floors and walls.

They imply the city once had an energy network.

Block variants:

dead_lumen_conduit
dim_lumen_conduit
active_lumen_conduit

Initial behavior:

Dead conduit emits no light.
Dim conduit emits light level 2.
Active conduit emits light level 8.

In ruined cities, most conduits are dead or dim.

Later gameplay use:

Powering ancient doors
Activating vaults
Stabilizing portals
Cosmetic base lighting
2. Resonance Cores

A Resonance Core is an ancient machine block.

Appearance:

Dark metallic/crystalline frame
Suspended glowing crystal center
Broken variants have cracked glass and flickering particles

Generated variants:

dormant_resonance_core
cracked_resonance_core
active_resonance_core

Initial behavior:

Mostly decorative
Emits faint sound/particles
Rarely appears in central chambers or vault rooms

Future behavior:

Used for advanced crafting
Portal stabilization
Gravity-related devices
City restoration mechanics
3. Memory Crystals

Memory Crystals are lore blocks.

Appearance:

Tall translucent crystal shards
Pulsing internal light
Glyph-like particles

Behavior:

Can be right-clicked.
Displays short fragmented lore text.
Some are broken and unreadable.
Rare intact ones reveal hints about the Lumenwrights.

Example messages:

The crystal hums, but the memory is incomplete.
A city of light beneath a moon that never slept.
They grew their roads from stone and taught the roots to sing.
The sky engines failed first.
The lower vaults were sealed before the final Sporefall.

These should be short and mysterious.

4. Gravity Lenses

Gravity Lenses are advanced alien devices found in spires and transit areas.

Appearance:

Ring-like block structure
Shimmerstone frame
suspended blue-violet lens
floating particles above it

Initial behavior:

Decorative or lightly functional.
Emits particles.
Occasionally applies a very weak upward drift or slow-fall effect near intact versions.

Future use:

Low-gravity elevators
Jump pads
Spire traversal
Crafting Lightfoot gear
5. Lumen Rail Channels

These are not minecart rails. They are ancient transit lines.

Appearance:

Smooth recessed channels in floors
Dim conduit lines
broken segments crossing plazas

They imply the Lumenwrights moved through the city using light/gravity transit.

Initial behavior:

Decorative path blocks.
Can guide players through the city.

Future behavior:

Speed boost paths
Particle trails
Restored city traversal systems
6. Dormant Light Engines

Large city-scale machines.

Appearance:

multi-block ruin centerpiece
circular glowbrick base
cracked crystal housing
dead conduit lines radiating outward

Location:

central plaza
underground power chamber
top of spire in grand cities

Behavior:

Usually dead.
Rare intact versions flicker.
Emits low hum.
May unlock vaults later.

These are the strongest visual clue that the city was once technologically advanced.

Ruin Decay Rules

The ruins must not look freshly generated or intact.

Each city uses decay patterns.

Block Replacement

Original clean blocks are partially replaced:

Glowbrick Block -> Cracked Glowbrick Block
Cracked Glowbrick Block -> Ancient Glowbrick Block
Glowbrick Tiles -> broken/missing sections
Sporeglass -> Broken Sporeglass
Moonstone Bricks -> Cracked/Mossy/Rooted Moonstone
Conduit blocks -> Dead or Dim Conduits
Missing Chunks

Buildings are incomplete.

Common damage:

missing roofs
collapsed floors
broken bridges
half-standing arches
exposed interiors

Overgrowth

Nature has reclaimed the cities.

Add:

Moonloam patches over floors

Light Decay

Lighting tells the story.

Fully intact Glowbricks glow softly.
Cracked Glowbricks glow weakly.
Ancient Glowbricks barely glow.
Some conduits flicker.
Some chambers are dead dark.
Vaults may still pulse with active light.

The player should feel like the city still has a heartbeat, but it is fading.

New City-Specific Hostile Mob

Introduce one dedicated city mob:

Echo Sentinel

The Echo Sentinel is a broken ancient guardian.

Appearance
Floating or hovering construct
Shattered glowbrick/metal shell
Lumen crystal eye
Broken ring segments orbiting it
Flickering teal light
Behavior
Spawns rarely in underground vaults and spire cores.
Dormant until the player gets close or opens certain chests.
Attacks with short-range light pulses.
Moves slowly but ignores some terrain.
Flickers between active and unstable states.
Combat Identity

The Echo Sentinel is not a skeleton with a new texture. It is a ruin guardian.

Attack pattern:

Charges briefly.
Emits a visible glow.
Fires a short pulse or beam-like projectile.
Pauses while its core flickers.
Repositions slowly.
Drops
Resonance Core Fragment
Luminite Ingot
Memory Crystal Shard
Rare Lumen Relay
Purpose

The Echo Sentinel proves the ancient technology is not completely dead.

The city is ruined, but some systems still remember how to defend it.

Traps and Environmental Hazards

Vestige Cities use ancient failing technology as hazards.

Flicker Floors

Some glowbrick floors flicker.

Behavior:

Look stable.
Briefly pulse before collapsing or opening.
Drop the player into lower chambers.

Implementation-friendly version:

Use cracked blocks and hidden holes rather than real timed collapse at first.
Unstable Gravity Wells

Found in spires and engineering chambers.

Behavior:

Applies brief low gravity or upward push.
Can throw off jumps.
Can help reach loot if used carefully.
Dead Light Zones

Some rooms are unnaturally dark.

Behavior:

Natural glowing plants are absent.
Shade Stalkers spawn more easily.
Dead conduits line the walls.
Spore-Sealed Chambers

Overgrown rooms in jungle variants.

Behavior:

Breaking spore growth may release Sporelings.
Loot is partially buried in organic material.
Lumen Shock Conduits

Rare active conduits.

Behavior:

Damage or knock back entities that stand on them.
Can be disabled later with the right tool or item.

What the Player Learns From the Ruins

The ruins should imply a story without over-explaining it.

The player should infer:

The Lumenwrights built with Glowbrick and Luminite.
They controlled light as infrastructure.
They had gravity technology.
Their cities were connected by conduit networks.
Something caused those networks to fail.
Nature reclaimed the cities.
Some machines are still partially alive.
The Lumenwilds was once inhabited by a civilization far more advanced than the player.
That civilization is gone.
The player is walking through the remains, not conquering an active society.

Final Definition

Vestige Cities are rare ruined alien cities built by the vanished Lumenwrights. They are made from Glowbrick, Luminite, Shimmerstone, Lumen Crystal, and living-light technology. They are circular, vertical, overgrown, partially collapsed, and filled with dead conduits, broken gravity devices, memory crystals, resonance cores, and flickering machines.

They are not just loot structures.

They are proof that the Lumenwilds had a civilization.

And that civilization is gone.
