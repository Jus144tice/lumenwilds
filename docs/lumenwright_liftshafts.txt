Lumenwright Liftshafts

The ancient alien cities should contain functional Lumenwright Liftshafts: paired gravity columns used to move between the surface city and deep mining chambers below.

Each mining access point has two shafts:

Descent Shaft — pulls entities downward in a controlled fall.
Ascension Shaft — pushes entities upward through antigravity.

The player sees these as two vertical glowing columns side by side, usually inside a ruined mining transit building or under a broken city plaza.

The visual message is:

This civilization mined vertically, but they did not climb. They floated.

Minecraft 1.21 has a built-in gravity attribute exposed through entity attributes, and NeoForge documents attributes as synced double values used for entity properties, with custom attributes also possible. That said, the most reliable implementation for shaft blocks is probably not “change the player’s global gravity,” but “apply controlled vertical velocity while the player is inside a shaft volume.”

The Structure: Mine Access Dais

In Vestige Cities, the lift system should generate as a special structure module called:

lumenwilds:vestige_mine_access_dais

It appears in:

medium Vestige Cities
grand Vestige Cities
cracked spire cities
underground Vestige substructures
rare overgrown city variants

It should not appear in every ruin. When it does appear, it feels like a major discovery.

Surface Layout

The mine access dais is a circular or octagonal platform built from:

Glowbrick Tiles
Chiseled Glowbrick
Polished Shimmerstone
Dim Lumen Conduits
Ancient Glowbrick
Lumen Crystal fragments
Broken Gravity Lens blocks

At the center are two vertical shafts, side by side.

[ DESCENT SHAFT ]   [ ASCENSION SHAFT ]

The Descent Shaft has downward-flowing particles.

The Ascension Shaft has upward-flowing particles.

Both are surrounded by partial rings of Glowbrick, like the remains of an ancient elevator station.

Shaft Blocks

Do not make these actual fluids. Make them non-solid field blocks, similar in spirit to portal blocks, bubble columns, or tripwire-like invisible logic blocks.

Descent Field

Block ID:

lumenwilds:descent_field

Behavior:

No collision.
Transparent or semi-transparent.
Emits light level 5.
Applies controlled downward velocity.
Caps falling speed.
Resets or reduces fall distance.
Prevents lethal fall damage while inside the shaft.
Allows the player to exit sideways at any point.

This should not feel like falling. It should feel like being lowered by gravity technology.

Recommended behavior:

If entity is inside Descent Field:
- If vertical speed is greater than -0.35, push downward slightly.
- If vertical speed is less than -0.45, slow it back upward slightly.
- Set fallDistance to 0 or heavily reduce it.

So the player descends at a steady, safe speed.

Ascension Field

Block ID:

lumenwilds:ascension_field

Behavior:

No collision.
Transparent or semi-transparent.
Emits light level 7.
Applies upward velocity.
Caps upward speed.
Lets the player float upward smoothly.
Sneaking should slow or stop ascent.
Jumping should give a small upward boost.

Recommended behavior:

If entity is inside Ascension Field:
- Apply upward motion until vertical speed reaches about +0.32 to +0.45.
- If sneaking, reduce upward velocity or hold position.
- Reset fallDistance.

This gives players control without making the shaft annoying.

Why This Is Better Than True Gravity Reversal

You could technically experiment with gravity attributes, but for this feature I would avoid making the shaft work by constantly rewriting the player’s actual gravity value.

The safer Minecraft implementation is:

The shaft is a column of blocks that directly manipulates entity motion while entities are inside it.

That is easier to debug, easier to confine to the shaft, works on players and mobs, and avoids weird side effects with jump physics, elytra, projectiles, mounts, or other mods.

Save actual gravity attribute manipulation for broader dimension-wide low gravity or specific gear later.

For the liftshafts, use field blocks.

The shafts should not look like water elevators or bubble columns. They should look like alien infrastructure.

Rings pulsing upward.

The Ascension Shaft should feel magical and technological.

Eventually they craft a Lumen Field Projector.

Lumen Field Projector Recipe

Suggested recipe:

G L G
R C R
I E I

Where:

G = Gravity Lens Fragment
L = Lumen Crystal Shard
R = Lumen Relay
C = Resonance Core Fragment
I = Luminite Ingot
E = Echo Shard

Output:

1 Lumen Field Projector

This makes it advanced and tied to ruins.

Projector Modes

The projector has two modes:

Ascend
Descend

The player changes the mode by right-clicking with an empty hand, or by shift-right-clicking.

Mode is shown in the block tooltip:

Mode: Ascension
Mode: Descent
How It Builds the Field

A Lumen Field Projector projects a vertical column above itself.

Default range:

16 blocks

Upgraded range later:

32 blocks

It fills the column with temporary or generated field behavior, not necessarily permanent placed blocks.

Simpler first implementation:

Projector checks the vertical column above it.
If air or replaceable blocks exist, it places invisible/transparent ascension_field or descent_field blocks.
Breaking the projector removes the field blocks.
Field blocks tick/check for their projector source and disappear if unsupported.

Cleaner later implementation:

Field is not placed as blocks.
Projector uses an area effect check each tick.
Entities inside the vertical AABB are moved.

For Minecraft mod simplicity, I’d start with actual field blocks. They are easier to see, debug, save, and interact with.

City Generation With Cave Detection

Yes, the generative piece is doable, but there are two levels of complexity.

NeoForge mods can ship data-pack worldgen files, including dimension and worldgen resources, as part of the mod data pack; NeoForge’s docs list dimension, dimension_type, structure, and worldgen as data-side resource folders. For structures, a data-driven jigsaw/NBT approach works well for modular ruins, and structure tutorials for modern NeoForge show that NBT pieces, template pools, loot-table chests, and custom Java behavior can be combined.

Simple Version

The city generates a mine access shaft whether or not a real cave is below.

The shaft descends to a generated abandoned mine chamber.

That chamber is part of the structure.

This is much easier and more reliable.

Generation flow:

Generate city
  -> choose mine access module
  -> generate vertical shaft
  -> generate abandoned mine chamber at fixed depth below

This guarantees the content exists.

Downside:

It may not always connect to a natural cave.
It feels slightly less organic.
Better Version

The city attempts to detect a cave below.

Generation flow:

Generate city candidate
  -> scan downward below mine access point
  -> look for a cave-sized air pocket
  -> if cave exists:
       generate liftshaft down to cave
       generate abandoned mine attachments in cave
     else:
       generate normal city piece without mine access

This is doable, but it requires custom structure placement/generation logic rather than pure static JSON.

Recommended Version

Use a hybrid:

First, try to connect to a real cave.
If no suitable cave is found, generate a buried artificial mine chamber.

This gives the best player experience.

The player always gets a cool mining shaft when the module appears, but sometimes it naturally intersects an existing cave system, which feels amazing.

Cave Detection Rule

When generating a Vestige City with a mine access dais, pick a shaft origin point near the city plaza.

Then scan downward.

Candidate Cave Requirements

A valid cave below should satisfy:

- Between 20 and 80 blocks below the city floor
- Contains enough air blocks to count as an open space
- Has at least one solid floor nearby
- Is not fully submerged unless generating a sunken variant
- Has enough horizontal room for abandoned mining decorations

Implementation-style logic:

For y from surfaceY - 20 down to minBuildHeight + 20:
    sample a 7x7x5 volume around shaftX, y, shaftZ

    airCount = count air/cave blocks
    solidFloorCount = count solid blocks near bottom

    if airCount > threshold and solidFloorCount > threshold:
        caveY = y
        foundCave = true
        break

Then generate the shaft from surfaceY down to caveY.

Ruined City Integration

The liftshafts should be visibly part of the city’s infrastructure.

In Small Ruins

Only broken shaft remnants:

cracked circular platform
dead conduits
no working field
loot maybe includes one Gravity Lens Fragment
In Medium Cities

One working or partially working shaft pair:

Descent shaft works
Ascension shaft flickers
connects to small abandoned mine
In Grand Cities

Multiple shaft systems:

civic liftshafts between city levels
mine access shafts
spire ascension columns
one sealed deep shaft leading to a vault
In Cracked Spire Cities

Gravity tech is more prominent:

vertical traversal puzzles
broken ascension fields
floating debris
Crag Wraiths around exposed shafts
In Sunken Cities

The liftshafts may be flooded or damaged:

Descent shaft becomes dangerous because water interrupts field stability
Ascension shaft bubbles with Lumenwater
Mirelurkers hide near shaft exits
Gameplay Payoff

This feature gives the Lumenwilds a fantastic progression loop.

First Encounter

Player finds a ruined city and sees two glowing shafts.

They step into the Descent Shaft and slowly sink downward.

They realize:

This is an elevator, but it is made of gravity.

Exploration

At the bottom, they discover an abandoned alien mining chamber.

They find:

Luminite Ore
Gravity Lens Fragments
dead machinery
loot caches
lore
maybe an Echo Sentinel
Reward

Eventually they craft their own Lumen Field Projector.

Now they can build:

base elevators
mine elevators
tower lifts
floating garden access
low-gravity transit shafts
vertical city builds
safe drops into deep caves

This is exactly the kind of technology players should want to bring home from the Lumenwilds.
